# How to cure a gourd mate

A new gourd mate is cured before its first use: fill it with used, damp yerba, add hot water without letting it boil and let it rest for about 24 hours. The next day, empty it and scrape off the soft skin inside. Repeat for two or three days and it is ready.

Ceramic and steel mates don't need curing. A rinse with hot water is enough.

## Why a mate is cured

The gourd is a natural material. Inside it has a soft layer that, if left untouched, gives the first mates a bitter, odd taste, and it can crack or grow mould. Curing removes that layer, settles the gourd and leaves the inner wall ready for yerba every day.

## Step by step

1. **Fill it with used, damp yerba.** Up to the top, pressed down but not packed too hard.
2. **Add hot water, without letting it boil.** The same water you would use to drink mate.
3. **Let it rest for about 24 hours.** In a cool place, out of the sun.
4. **Empty it and scrape off the soft skin** with a spoon, taking care not to damage the rim or the leather.
5. **Repeat the process for two or three days.**
6. **Let it dry upside down** in a well-ventilated place before your first mate.

## Common mistakes

- **Using boiling water:** it burns the gourd and can crack it.
- **Leaving it damp after use:** this is the main cause of mould. Always empty it and leave it upside down.
- **Washing it with detergent:** the taste stays in the gourd. Water is enough.
- **Scraping with something sharp:** a spoon does the job.

## Care after curing

After every mate, empty it, rinse it with water and let it dry upside down in a ventilated place, without detergent. If your mate is covered in leather, the leather needs its own care: we explain it in [how to care for the leather of your mate and matera](/en/guides/leather-care).

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to cure a mate?**
Two or three days, repeating the damp yerba process every 24 hours.

**Do ceramic or steel mates need curing?**
No. After a rinse with hot water they are ready to use.
